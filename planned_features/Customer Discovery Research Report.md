# Customer Discovery Research Report: Synthetic Survey Research Product (US, CPG/Agency Focus)

**Bottom Line Up Front:** A $2,000 LLM-powered concept-screening product positioned as a *funnel pre-filter* (200 → 10 concepts) ahead of human panels has a defensible wedge in the US CPG and agency market in 2026 — but only if it is sold to mid-market CPG insights/innovation managers and brand-strategy/innovation consultancies (not enterprise CPG procurement), priced just under typical PO-approval thresholds, and positioned explicitly as "directional pre-screening that supplements (not replaces) panel research." The competitive landscape has bifurcated into well-funded enterprise synthetic vendors (Aaru at a ~$1B Series A headline valuation; Evidenza targeting B2B; Fairgen targeting boost/augmentation) and traditional concept-test platforms (Zappi, Quantilope at $22K+ entry, Suzy on credit-based models, Remesh, SurveyMonkey) — leaving a clear gap at the *low-cost, fast, "kill obvious losers"* tier where the SSR method (Maier et al., arXiv:2510.08338, Oct 2025) is methodologically credible because it achieves ~90% of human test-retest reliability on purchase intent on a 57-survey personal-care dataset.

## TL;DR

- **The wedge is real but narrow.** A $2K screening tool fits the gap between $99 self-serve survey tools and $15K–$200K full panel studies, especially given CPG concept-test costs typically run $15K–$75K per study (User Intuition reference data) and top-tier alternatives like Quantilope start at $22,000/year for the entry "Business" plan. Sell it as "go from 200 concepts to 10" — the explicit brief — not as a panel replacement, because every credible practitioner critique (Kantar, Ipsos, NIM, Verstaresearch, ESOMAR Code 2025) insists on synthetic-as-supplement.
- **Sell first to mid-market CPG insights managers, brand-strategy/innovation consultancies, and DTC brands** — not to large CPG procurement (where any new vendor triggers IT/legal/MSA cycles) and not to ad agencies (where the buyer is a strategist with no research budget). Insights/innovation managers at $200M–$2B CPGs typically control $200K–$2M discretionary research budgets and can approve $2K via expense report or a single-PO workflow without committee.
- **Pricing recommendation: hold at $1,995 per study with a 5-pack at $7,500.** $2K is the right "test-and-learn" price; $5K signals a serious project that procurement will gate; sub-$1K signals "toy." The single biggest risk is *not* price — it's the skepticism wall (academic critiques of LLM survey simulation are accumulating fast in 2024–2026), so the strongest go-to-market move is a head-to-head validation case study against a known winning/losing concept set, modeled on Evidenza's "EY Global Brand Survey" pitch where the synthetic and human results were "95% the same."

## SECTION 1 — Buyer Mapping (US CPG, Agencies, Adjacent Orgs)

Evidence strength: **STRONG** for role taxonomy and reporting lines (LinkedIn job postings, User Intuition CPG team-structure reference); **MODERATE** for budget authority thresholds (no published industry benchmark; estimates triangulated from User Intuition and vendor anecdotes).

### Inside large/mid-market CPG (the primary "concept decision" roles)

**1. Senior Consumer Insights Manager / Sr. Insights Manager — CPG**
- *Sits in:* Consumer Insights function, usually reporting to a Brand/Category Insights Director, who reports to a VP Consumer Insights or Chief Insights Officer. Often dotted-line to the relevant brand/marketing GM.
- *Authority:* Per User Intuition's CPG team-structure reference, Senior Insights Managers "design and lead individual studies, manage vendor relationships for specific projects" and "hold 5–10 years of research experience and manage 3–6 concurrent projects." They are the workhorse buyer for ad-hoc concept tests.
- *Budget:* Typically draws against a brand/category research budget owned by the Category/Brand Insights Director (User Intuition cites $2M–$5M annual category research budgets at $10B CPGs). They commonly have authority to issue single POs in the $5K–$25K range without committee approval; above that, sign-off elevates.
- *Procurement:* For a NEW vendor, almost always must route through an MSA/IT-security/data-privacy review at large enterprises (P&G, Unilever, PepsiCo). At mid-market CPGs, they can often onboard a vendor via expense report or P-card under ~$5K.
- *Real listing example:* Starbucks "Market Research & Insights Senior Manager – CPG" (LinkedIn JD): "Independently design, execute, analyze and report custom research projects… leveraging your deep knowledge of research methods, analytics and synthesis to build compelling, persuasive cases for action."

**2. Category/Brand Insights Director**
- *Sits in:* Reports to VP Insights; partners with a brand GM/marketing director.
- *Authority:* Per User Intuition reference, "owns the consumer knowledge agenda for a specific brand, category, or business unit" and "translates business challenges into research questions, design[s] annual learning plans" — they are the budget-holder above the manager.
- *Budget:* $2M–$5M annual research budget; typically approve single studies up to $25K–$100K without escalation.
- *Most likely champion* for a $2K experimental tool — they have the discretionary budget *and* the strategic mandate to test new methods.

**3. VP Consumer Insights / Chief Insights Officer**
- *Sits in:* Reports to CMO, COO, or Chief Growth Officer.
- *Authority:* Owns the function-level vendor stack. Will not personally choose a $2K tool but is the *blocker or enabler* — must be socialized.
- *Budget:* Function-level discretionary; greenlights pilots that managers source.

**4. Innovation Director / Head of Innovation / R&D Insights Lead**
- *Sits in:* Either inside Insights, inside R&D, or in a standalone "Innovation" function reporting to CMO/Chief Growth Officer/Chief Innovation Officer. Highly variable.
- *Authority:* Owns the new-product pipeline — concept ideation, screening, validation, launch. Per Dig Insights/Mission Field framing, this role "owns getting from a long list of concepts to a short list."
- *Budget:* Often a separate "innovation" budget distinct from insights. NIQ data: the average CPG innovation costs $15M to launch, and only 11% retain customers after year one — innovation directors are under intense pressure to kill bad concepts early. They are the *most pain-aware* buyer for concept screening.
- *Procurement:* Same gate as insights for new vendors; but in an innovation function, more cultural openness to "experimental methods."

**5. Senior Manager, Strategy & Insights / Foresight / Trends Lead**
- *Sits in:* Strategy or Insights function. Smaller in headcount than core Insights Managers but disproportionately influential.
- *Authority/Budget:* Lower direct budget but high influence; often sets methodology standards.

**6. Category Management / Shopper Insights**
- *Sits in:* Often inside the sales organization (e.g., Customer Development), separate from consumer insights.
- *Concept testing relevance:* Lower priority for the SSR product — they care more about retailer-facing data than consumer concept reads. Deprioritize.

**7. Knowledge Management / Insights Operations Lead**
- *Sits in:* Centralized Insights function. Per User Intuition: "ratio of research ops to total team size should be approximately 1:8–10."
- *Relevance:* They control panel/vendor procurement infrastructure. Important to understand but not the primary buyer.

### Inside agencies and consultancies

**8. Director/VP of Strategy (Brand-Strategy Agencies, e.g., Interbrand, Lippincott, Siegel+Gale, Prophet)**
- *Authority:* Often controls project research budgets within client engagements; typically can approve $2K–$10K research line-items inside a project budget.
- *Procurement:* Loose — they bill the client through, often as a pass-through.

**9. Senior Strategist / Group Strategy Director (Ad Agencies)**
- *Authority:* Generally **does NOT control research budget**. Research is brokered through a client-services lead and ultimately paid by the client. Lower priority buyer because the procurement path is opaque.

**10. Innovation Consultancy Partner / Director (e.g., IDEO, Doblin, ?What If!, Frog, Lippincott Innovation, BCG Brightpoint, Fahrenheit 212-style firms)**
- *Authority:* High — they routinely bring new methods into client engagements and bill them as a line item.
- *Best-fit buyer profile:* These consultancies actively curate "new methods." Likely first to adopt synthetic screening as a tool to expand internal capability without raising headcount.

**11. Packaging/Industrial Design Firm Leadership (e.g., Smith & Diction, Pearlfisher, Sterling Brands)**
- *Authority/Budget:* Very low research budget; usually outsource testing entirely. Smaller TAM.

### Where evidence is thin

- **Exact dollar thresholds for "manager-level PO authority"** are not publicly published per company. Triangulated estimate: **$2,500–$5,000 is the typical credit-card/expense-report ceiling** at large enterprises; $5K–$10K is the typical single-PO threshold without committee sign-off. This is the single most important number to validate during discovery calls — ask it directly.
- LinkedIn JD scrapes for innovation roles vary widely; "Innovation Manager" titles inside CPGs do not have a standardized org placement.

- *Methodology:* Agent-based; not LLM-pure. Famously predicted 2024 NY Democratic primary within 371 votes (per Semafor).
- *Pricing:* Not public; targeting enterprise.

**Evidenza** (founded by Peter Weinberg, Jon Lombardo — formerly LinkedIn B2B Institute — and Brian Watroba ex-Facebook; Mark Ritson is advisor + minority shareholder)

### The gap analysis: where does $2K AI concept screening fit?

The market is **two-tier**, with a wide chasm:

- **Tier 1 (cheap, fast, weak rigor):** SurveyMonkey templates, raw self-serve panels at $1–$5/respondent → ~$500–$2,000 per study but methodologically thin.
- **Tier 2 (rigorous, slow, expensive):** Zappi/Quantilope/Suzy/Remesh at $10K–$50K+ per study (or $22K+/year subscription).
- **Synthetic enterprise tier:** Aaru/Evidenza/Fairgen/Yabble at custom enterprise pricing, mostly $20K–$200K+ engagements.

**The wedge:** A $2,000 SSR-method screener positioned as **"Tier 1 price, Tier 2 directional rigor, used as a pre-screen before Tier 2"** has white space — *especially* with the Maier et al. 2025 SSR paper as methodological anchor (90% of human test-retest reliability on a 9,300-response personal-care dataset; Pearson correlation of ranked concept means is high).

Fairgen Twins ("decisions that can't justify a $10K study") is the closest direct competitor in positioning. Yabble's $1,500 promo is the closest direct competitor in price. **Strong evidence the wedge exists; moderate evidence it's defensible** — the wedge is narrow because (a) Fairgen and Yabble can drop prices, and (b) Quantilope/Zappi could spin up cheap synthetic tiers. **Speed of land-and-expand and methodological credibility (lean hard on the SSR paper) are the only durable moats.**

## SECTION 5 — Skepticism Map

Evidence strength: **STRONG** — academic and trade-press critique is heavily documented and rapidly growing.

### Academic critiques (will be cited at you in calls)

- **Bisbee et al. (2024)** — "synthetic survey replacements can produce misleading results on novel political questions, particularly when the question format or content departs from the model's training distribution."
- **Santurkar et al. (2023)** — broadly cited as the foundational critique of LLM survey simulation (LLMs reproduce dominant training-data opinions, not minority views).
- **Verstaresearch.com summary of recent academic study** (Mar 2026): "Our findings raise serious concerns about the quality, reliability, and reproducibility of synthetic survey data generated by LLMs. … There was less variation in scores compared to the real survey; correlations and regression coefficients did not match the real survey; identical questions yielded significantly different results when replicated over a 3-month period."
- **arXiv 2510.19952 ("Synthetic social data: trials and tribulations," 2025)** — "even a small, skewed sample of real respondents may provide more reliable insights than synthetic data produced by LLMs."
- **arXiv 2509.09871 (González-Bustamante et al., 2025)** — Chilean public-opinion benchmark; "downstream applications risk reproducing social stereotypes and biases inherited from training data."
- **Counter-evidence — your strongest defense:** **Maier et al., arXiv:2510.08338 (Oct 9, 2025)**, "LLMs Reproduce Human Purchase Intent via Semantic Similarity Elicitation of Likert Ratings" — on a real corporate dataset of **57 personal-care surveys with 9,300 human responses**, SSR achieves **90% of human test-retest reliability** with KS similarity >0.85. Cite this paper *constantly*.

### Practitioner critiques

- **Kantar** ("What is synthetic sample"): blended human + synthetic models will become "more common as LLMs get even more powerful," but "off-the-shelf" LLMs cannot meet brief.
- **Ipsos** (April 2024 paper, "LLMs in market research"): MaxDiff studies "more encouraging," but persona-based synthetic results variable; "current inability to fully capture the complexity and variability of human behaviour."
- **NIM** (Nuremberg Institute for Market Decisions): Tested 500 GPT-4 digital-twin respondents vs. 500 real US consumers across soft drinks, sportswear, US politics; concluded synthetic is "supplemental, not a replacement: While AI can provide helpful directional insights, it lacks the precision and diversity needed for actionable consumer insights."
- **NIQ** (NielsenIQ, 2024 article): explicit warning about "convincing—but sometimes unsubstantiated—output"; recommends key vetting questions.
- **Heineken** (per ESOMAR Reimagine 2025 recap): risk hierarchy — Step 0 (descriptive personas) = no risk, Step 1 (data imputation/boosting) = low risk, Step 2+ (LLM-generated personas, digital twins) = higher risk requiring validation.
- **Versta Research blog (March 2026)**: blunt headline: *"Can You Really Use AI to Create 'Synthetic' Survey Respondents? Just-Published Academic Research Says No."*

### Formal industry positioning

- **ICC/ESOMAR International Code 2025 update** (adopted June 2025): first major Code revision in nearly a decade specifically addressing AI and synthetic data. Per Joaquim Bretcha, ESOMAR Director General: *"New principles addressing AI and synthetic data: Clear definitions and responsibilities around AI, synthetic data, and synthetic personas, with a new emphasis on transparency and human oversight."* ESOMAR has also published "20 Questions to help buyers of AI-based services for market research" — your sales team must have an on-page response to all 20.
- **Insights Association** updated its Code in 2024 to include "artificial intelligence" definitions and a new "Data and technology" section. (No formal IA position banning synthetic respondents; the IA has been welcoming of synthetic-themed sessions at its Annual Conference.)
- **MRS (UK, but US-relevant for global brands)**: published a three-part series on generative AI's impact on the research sector, including synthetic respondents and adoption challenges.

### The most-cited objections you will face — and rehearsed responses

| Objection | Best response / framing |
|---|---|
| "LLMs just regurgitate training data" | The SSR method (Maier et al. 2025) decouples generation from scale-mapping; embedding-based cosine similarity to anchors avoids the LLM rating itself. Cite Vera Pichardo (arXiv:2602.13862) calibration study. |

### The procurement-gate threshold (the most important number)

Triangulating from User Intuition guidance, vendor sales blog discussions, and standard enterprise procurement practice (no single published source on this):

- **Under $2,500** — Often expense-reportable by an insights manager without PO; "credit-card friendly."
- **$2,500–$5,000** — Single-PO, often manager-approved at mid-market firms; usually still requires director sign-off at large CPGs.
- **$5,000–$10,000** — Director approval; vendor onboarding starts; legal review for new vendors.
- **$10,000+** — Procurement involvement; MSA likely required at large enterprises.
- **$25,000+** — Committee approval; often annual budget cycle.

### Defensible pricing recommendation

**Hold at $1,995 per study** with these structural choices:

1. **List the price publicly** — most synthetic vendors hide pricing, which signals "enterprise sales cycle." A published $1,995 signals "no-friction trial." This is your differentiator versus Aaru/Evidenza.
2. **Offer a 5-pack at $7,500 ($1,500/study, 25% off)** — encourages portfolio screening behavior; still under typical $10K committee threshold.
3. **Offer an annual subscription at $25,000 for 20 studies** ($1,250/study, 37.5% off) — falls just *above* Quantilope's $22K Business floor, deliberately positioning as "the same scale of commitment but for screening rather than full studies."
4. **Cap study size at 200 concepts** — protects unit economics and prevents commoditization; aligns with the "200 → 10" pitch.
5. **Free pilot study (1–2 concepts)** for first 50 customers to seed case studies. The single biggest objection-killer is a head-to-head case study against a known winning vs. losing concept.

**Why not lower?** $499–$999 puts you in SurveyMonkey territory and signals "toy." Insights managers will not stake their reputation on a tool that costs less than an Adobe subscription. The Yabble $1,500 promo proves the floor; $1,995 sits a notch above and reads as professional.

**Why not higher?** $5,000 puts you over the discretionary-PO threshold at most mid-market CPGs and lengthens the sales cycle from days to months. You lose the "swipe-able" velocity that's your only structural advantage versus Evidenza/Aaru.

**The single biggest risk to this pricing:** If any of Yabble, Fairgen Twins, or a new entrant publishes a sub-$1,000 self-serve tier in 2026 with an SSR-comparable methodology, the wedge collapses. Watch their pricing pages monthly.

## Caveats

- **The Maier et al. SSR paper (arXiv:2510.08338) was published Oct 2025 and revised through Oct 27, 2025**; it has not yet been peer-reviewed. A separate methodological critique by Eduardo Vera Pichardo (arXiv:2602.13862) extends and validates it but is also a working paper. Buyers in regulated categories (pharma, financial) will discount working papers; lean on the corporate-data N=9,300 result rather than methodological elegance.
- **All competitor pricing not on a published page is triangulated** from G2/Capterra/Vendr/Attest/FishDog reviews and customer testimonials; expect 30–50% variance in actual quoted prices. Do not quote competitor pricing in customer calls without the source.
- **Aaru's $1B "headline" valuation is multi-tier** per TechCrunch — actual blended valuation is below $1B, and ARR is reportedly under $10M. Treat valuation press as enterprise-confidence-building, not as evidence of product superiority.
- **Reddit r/marketresearch and ResearchOps Slack quotes were not surfaceable** in public web search — discovery calls are the only verification path. Ask explicitly: "Where do you and your peers vent about research vendors?"
- **GRIT 2025's 87%-positive synthetic-data adopter stat** comes from a self-selected sample of researchers who chose to use synthetic data — be careful presenting it as a market-wide signal.
- **The "200 concepts to 10" pitch number is a positioning claim**, not an empirical benchmark. Encourage early customers to publish their own funnel ratios (e.g., "we screened 87 concepts and advanced 9 to fieldwork") so you build a real distribution.
- **The Insights Association has not published a formal position paper specifically restricting synthetic respondents** as of search date. ESOMAR's June 2025 Code update is the closest thing to formal industry positioning and is broadly enabling rather than prohibitive — but emphasizes transparency. Have an "ESOMAR-aligned methodology disclosure" page ready before pitching enterprise CPGs.
- **Michael Shapiro retired from Nestlé Purina March 20, 2026** — confirm any successor names before outreach.
- **The synthetic research category is moving extremely fast** — Aaru's Series A closed Dec 2025; the SSR paper published Oct 2025; ESOMAR Code revised June 2025; Listen Labs raised $69M after a viral hiring stunt in 2025. Re-run this report every 90 days.
